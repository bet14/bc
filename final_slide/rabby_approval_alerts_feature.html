Approval Risk Alerts

One of the most dangerous actions in DeFi is granting a token approval. Once a contract is approved to spend a holder's tokens, it can move them at any time afterward. Rabby surfaces four mechanisms — described below with the modules and rules behind each — to keep that decision informed.

Spots when an approval would let a contract spend without limit, and offers a one-click way to set a specific amount instead.

2. Multi-Layer Risk Scoring

Scores every approval — token or NFT — by blending a server-side verdict with how much is at stake and how other holders have reacted to the same spender, then uses that score to highlight the riskiest ones first.

Server layer

A risk level returned directly by the backend for the spender or contract — converted into a numeric score that anchors the overall evaluation.

Spend layer

Derived from the USD value currently exposed to the spender:

  • Below $10,000 → scored as safe
  • Below $100,000 → scored as warning
  • Above that → scored as danger

User-behavior layer

Derived from how the wider holder base treats the same spender — comparing how many addresses revoked it against how many approved it:

  • Fewer than 10 revokes → safe
  • Revokes above 4× approvals → danger
  • Revokes above 2× approvals → warning
How the layers combine
The spend score and the user-behavior score are taken together, and both their highest value and their sum are kept alongside the server's score. This combined evaluation is what orders the approval list — so a spender with heavy exposure or a poor revoke-to-approve ratio surfaces above one that merely carries a server warning.

3. Contract Reputation

Shows whether the contract is verified, how long it has existed, and how other holders have treated it — right on the signing screen.

Unfamiliar contract warning: When encountering a contract or DApp interacted with for the first time, Rabby displays a yellow shield icon. This signals the holder to verify the contract's identity before approving it, reducing the risk of interacting with malicious or spoofed addresses.
